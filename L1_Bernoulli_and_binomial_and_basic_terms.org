* The Experiment
** Drawing beans or tossing coins
I have a bowl of red beans and a bowl of white beans. Let us
1. Count 50 of each
2. Mix them in the big bowl
3. Draw a bean
4. Observe the color and record 0 if it is white, 1 if it is red
5. Return the bean into the big bowl
6. Shake the bowl to mix everything up
7. Repeat steps 3 to 6 for n=30 times
8. Calculate the average of the recorded number
9. Calculate the variance $s^2 = \frac{\sum(\text{value} - \text{average})^2}{n-1}$
** Tossing coins
We have a bunch of coins. Let us
1. Do 30 coin tosses (3 people do 10 tosses each)
2. Record a 1 for head and 0 for tail
3. Calculate the average of the recorded number
4. Calculate the variance
* Terminology
** Terminology of probability
- *Sample space* $\Omega$ is the set of all possible outcomes
  - {red bean, white bean}, {head, tail}
- A *random variable* is not a /variable/ but a /function/ that maps each outcome to a real number.
  - It is a mapping $\Omega  \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$
  - It bridges the gap between verbal description and numeric quantity (allowing us to do math on it)
- An *event* is a set of outcomes
  - 5 or fewer heads in 10 coin tosses (X <= 5)
  - 2 red beans out of 3 draws (X = 2)
  - The second part of the statement is understood from the description of the experiment
  - Even if a simple mathematical relation like < > = cannot capture
    an event, you can always list the elements of the set of outcomes
    to define the event.
** Words have meaning
These three events are different
- Out of 10 coin tosses, getting 3 heads anywhere
- Out of 10 coin tosses, getting heads on 2nd, 5th, and 7th toss
- Out of 10 coin tosses, getting heads only on 2nd, 5th, and 7th toss
* The theory
** Bernoulli trial and Bernoulli distribution
- Only two possible *outcomes*, often called success and failure (but they are equivalent)
  - red bean and white bean, head and tail are the outcomes
  - 1 for success, 0 for failure
- The sum of the probabilities of all outcomes is 1 by definition
- If probability of success is $p$, that of failure must be $1-p$
- Mean $\mu = p$, Variance $\sigma^{2} = p (1 - p)$
** Binomial distribution
- The number of heads out of 30 coin tosses
- The number of red beans out of 30 draw
