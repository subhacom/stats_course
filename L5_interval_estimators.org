* Theory
** Estimator
"An /estimator/ is a statistic whose value depends on the particular sample drawn. The value of the estimator, called the /estimate/, is used to predict the value of a population parameter."
** Point estimators: mean
- Single-value estimates of a parameter like population mean, population variance, etc., from a sample
- Point estimator of population mean $\mu$ is $\bar{X} = \frac{\sum_{n} X_{i}}{n}$.
- An estimator whose expected value equals the population parameter is called an /unbiased/ estimator of the parameter. $E[\bar{X}] = \mu$, so it is an /unbiased/ estimator of the population mean
- The standard deviation in the sample mean is $\text{SD}(\bar{X}) = \sigma/\sqrt{n}$ - called /standard error of the mean/
** Point estimators: variance and standard deviation
- When population mean $\mu$ is known, $\sigma^{2} = E[(X_{i} - \mu)^{2}]$, so the estimator of population variance should be $\frac{\sum_{n}(X_{i} - \mu)^{2}}{n}$
- But we do not know $\mu$, and estimate it by $\bar{X}$
- The unbiased estimator is $S^{2} = \frac{\sum_{n}(X_{i} - \bar{X})^{2}}{n-1}$ so that $E(S^{2}) = \sigma^{2}$
** Percentiles of Gaussian random variables
- For any $\alpha$ between 0 and 1, $z_{\alpha}$ represents the $100 (1 - \alpha)$ percentile of the standard normal distribution
  - that is $1 - \alpha$ fraction of the data points are under $z_{\alpha}$
  - $P(Z > z_{\alpha}) = \alpha$
- Example: $z_{0.025} = 1.96$ => 2.5% of the time a standard normal rv will exceed 1.96
** Interval estimators
- "An /interval estimator/ of a population parameter is an interval (in the real line) that is predicted to contain the parameter."
- "The /confidence/ we ascribe to the interval is the probability that it will contain the parameter"
** Interval estimator of $\mu$ of normal population with known $\sigma^{2}$
- $z_{0.025} = 1.96$ means $P(Z > 1.96) = 0.025$.
- By symmetry of the Gaussian distribution this also means $P(Z < -1.96) = 0.025$
- Putting these together, $P(|Z| > 1.96) = 2 \times 0.025 = 0.05$
- Therefore $P(|Z| < 1.96) = 1 - 0.05 = 0.95$, i.e., 95% of the time the /absolute value of Z/ is less than or equal to 1.96
- Now we are estimating the population mean $\mu$ by $\bar{X}$. The standardized sample mean is $Z = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} = \frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sigma}(\bar{X} - \mu)$
- Thus $P(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sigma}|\bar{X} - \mu| \le 1.96) = 0.95$
- Or, $P(|\bar{X} - \mu| \le 1.96 \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}) = 0.95$
- Or $P(\bar{X} - 1.96 \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \le \mu \le \bar{X} + 1.96 \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}) = 0.95$
** Interval estimator of $\mu$ of normal population with known $\sigma^{2}$
- Or $P(\bar{X} - 1.96 \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \le \mu \le \bar{X} + 1.96 \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}) = 0.95$
- The interval $\bar{X} \pm 1.96\text{SEM}$ is called a 95% confidence interval estimator of the population mean
- If you repeat the sampling many times and compute $\bar{X}$ and estimate this interval, 95% of these intervals will contain the population mean
- *Note: it is incorrect to say that there is a 95% probability that the population mean will lie within 1.96 SEM of a single sample mean.*
** Interval estimator of $\mu$ of normal population with known $\sigma^{2}$
The $100 (1-\alpha)$ percent confidence interval estimator of the population mean is $\bar{X} \pm \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}z_{\alpha/2}$
