* Hypothesis testing for two factors
** Factorial design
- For one-way ANOVA we had only one categorical variable
- You want to see the effect of two categorical variables on a continuous variable - the first two are the independent variables and the last is the dependent variable (also called response)
- Examples:
  - Growth of two strains of a bacteria/cells in three different culture media
  - Production of two varieties of wheat in three different soil types (sandy, loamy, clay)
  - If race or gender influence starting salary of students
  - If major or college category influence starting salary of students
** Hypotheses to test for categorical variables A and B
1. Interaction effect (AxB): Are the effects of factor A and B independent of one another?
   - $H_{0}:$ There is no interaction between factors
   - $H_{1}:$ There is a significant interaction between factors
2. Main effect of factor A: Does the dependent variable differ across the levels of factor A, averaging over factor B?
   - $H_{0}:$ There is no effect of factor A on the response variable
   - $H_{1}:$ There is a significant effect of factor A on the response variable
3. Main effect of factor B: Does the dependent variable differ across the levels of factor B, averaging over factor A?
   - $H_{0}:$ There is no effect of factor B on the response variable
   - $H_{1}:$ There is a significant effect of factor B on the response variable

If there is a significant interaction, then ignore hypotheses for the main effect. Otherwise, proceed to test the main effects.
** Assumptions for 2-way ANOVA
- Independent observations
- Each group has approximately equal variance
- Data is approximately normally distributed
- Samples from different treatments are independent

** The rectangular arrangement of data
In the simplest case, we have one observation for each pair of levels

#+ATTR_LATEX: :align |l|c|c|c|
| Category/Factor/Level | Soil 1   | Soil 2  | Soil 3   |
|-----------------------+----------+---------+----------|
| Strain 1              | $X_{11}$ | $X_{12}$ | $X_{13}$ |
|-----------------------+----------+---------+----------|
| Strain 2              | $X_{21}$ | $X_{22}$ | $X_{23}$ |



** Formula for two-factor ANOVA
The underlying model: For row i, column j, $\mu_{ij} = \mu + \alpha_{i} + \beta_{j}$

\bigskip

Effect of factor A: $H_{0}: \mu_{1j} = \mu_{2j}  = \mu + \beta_{j}$, i.e., $\alpha_{1} = \alpha_{2} = 0$, $H_{1}:$ At least one of the strain effects is different from the others.

Effect of factor B: $H_{0}: \mu_{i1} = \mu_{i2} = \mu_{i3}  = \mu + \alpha_{i}$, i.e., $\beta_{1} = \beta_{2} = \beta_{3} = 0$, $H_{1}:$ At least one of the soil effects is different from the others.

With just one observation per cell we cannot measure variation within a treatment, nor can we check interaction between factors.

\bigskip

The variation can be partitioned into /sum of squares/ ($SS$).

$SS_{total} = SS_{A} + SS_{B} + SS_{E}$

|-----------------------+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Strain 2              | $X_{21}$                           | $X_{22}$                           | $X_{23}$                           | $X_{2.}=\frac{X_{21} + X_{22} + X_{23}}{3}$                              |
|-----------------------+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Soil means ($X_{.j}$) | $X_{.1}=\frac{X_{11} + X_{21}}{2}$ | $X_{.2}=\frac{X_{12} + X_{22}}{2}$ | $X_{.3}=\frac{X_{13} + X_{23}}{2}$ | $X_{..} = \frac{X_{11} + X_{12} + X_{13} + X_{21} + X_{22} + X_{23}}{6}$ |
#+LATEX: }



** Formula for two-factor ANOVA
- We can estimate $\sigma^{2}$ noting that the sum of squares of $N$ normal rvs is a chi-squared rv with $N$ degrees of freedom.
- We take the sum of squares of $a b$ standardized variables $\frac{X_{ij} - \mu_{ij}}{\sigma}$

  #+begin_export latex
  \begin{gather*}
  \frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{a}\sum_{j=1}^{b}(X_{ij} - \mu_{ij})^{2}\\
  = \frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{a}\sum_{j=1}^{b}(X_{ij} - (\mu + \alpha_{i} + \beta_{j}))^{2}\\
  = \frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{a}\sum_{j=1}^{b}(X_{ij} - \mu - \alpha_{i} - \beta_{j})^{2}
  \end{gather*}
  #+end_export

** Formula for two-factor ANOVA
- For $\alpha_{1} \cdots \alpha_{a}$ we need to estimate $a - 1$ parameters, the $a$ -th $\alpha$ is already determined by the others because $\sum_{i=1}^{a}\alpha_{i} = 0$
- For $\beta_{1} \cdots \beta_{b}$ we need to estimate $b - 1$ parameters, the $b$ -th $\beta$ is already determined by the others because $\sum_{i=1}^{b}\beta_{i} = 0$
- For estimating $\mu$ we use 1 degree of freedom
- From a total $ab$ dof, we are losing $1 + (a-1) + (b-1) = a + b - 1$ dof to estimate all these parameters
- $\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{a} \sum_{j=1}^{b} (X_{ij} - \hat{\mu} - \hat{\alpha}_{i} - \hat{\beta}_{j})^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}$ is a chi-square rv with $ab - (a + b -1) = (a - 1)(b-1)$ dof

** Formula for two-factor ANOVA
#+begin_export latex
\begin{align*}
\hat{\mu} &= X_{..}\\
\hat{\alpha_{i}} &= X_{i.} - X_{..}\\
\hat{\beta_{j}} &= X_{.j} - X_{..}\\
\end{align*}
\text{thus}\\
\begin{align*}
\hat{\mu} + \hat{\alpha_{i}} + \hat{\beta_{j}} &= X_{..} + X_{i.} - X_{..} + X_{.j} - X_{..}\\
&= X_{i.} + X_{.j} - X_{..}
\end{align*}
#+end_export

** Formula for two-factor ANOVA
Thus, $\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{a} \sum_{j=1}^{b} (X_{ij} - \hat{\mu} - \hat{\alpha}_{i} - \hat{\beta}_{j})^{2}}{\sigma^{2}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{a} \sum_{j=1}^{b} (X_{ij} - X_{i.} - X_{.j} + X_{..})^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}$ is a chi-square rv with $\text{dof} = (a - 1)(b-1)$

\bigskip

We calculate sum of squared errors, $\text{SS}_{E} = \sum_{i=1}^{a} \sum_{j=1}^{b} (X_{ij} - X_{i.} - X_{.j} + X_{..})^{2}$

\bigskip

Since chi-square rv $X$ with $\text{dof}=n$ has $E[X] = n$,

\bigskip


$E\left[\frac{\text{SS}_{E}}{\sigma^{2}}\right] = (a - 1)(b - 1) \implies E[\frac{\text{SS}_{E}}{(a-1)(b-1)}] = \sigma^{2}$

\bigskip

Thus,  $\frac{\text{SS}_{E}}{(a-1)(b-1)}$ is an unbiased estimator of population variance $\sigma^{2}$

** Formula for two-factor ANOVA
When trying to test $H_{0}: \alpha_{i} = 0$ for all $i$, we can obtain a second estimator of $\sigma^{2}$.

Under $H_{0}$, $E[X_{i.}] = \mu + \alpha_{i} = \mu$

As $X_{i.}$ is the mean of $b$ random variables, each with variance $\sigma^{2}$, $\text{Var}(X_{i.}) = \frac{\sigma^{2}}{b}$

\bigskip

Thus, when $H_{0}$ is true, $\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{a}(X_{i.} - E[X_{i.}])^{2}}{\text{Var}(X_{i.})} = \frac{b}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{a}(X_{i.} - E[X_{i.}])^{2}$ is chi-squared with $\text{dof} = (a - 1)$

** Formula for two-factor ANOVA

We define $\text{SS}_{A} = b \sum_{i=1}^{a}(X_{i.} - E[X_{i.}])^{2}$

\bigskip

Then, $E\left[\frac{\text{SS}_{A}}{\sigma^{2}}\right] = a - 1 \implies E\left[\frac{\text{SS}_{A}}{a-1}\right] = \sigma^{2}$

\bigskip

Like one-way ANOVA, the ratio of these two estimators of $\sigma^{2}$ has the F-distribution with $(a-1)$ numerator-dof and $(a-1)(b-1)$ denominator-dof: $F_{a-1, (a-1)(b-1)}$

\bigskip

Thus, $\text{TS} = \frac{\text{SS}_{A}/(a-1)}{\text{SS}_{E}/((a-1)(b-1))} \sim F_{a-1, (a-1)(b-1)}$

Defining $\text{SS}_{A}/(a-1) = \text{MS}_{A}$, $\text{SS}_{E}/((a-1)(b-1)) = \text{MS}_{E}$, $TS = \frac{\text{MS}_{A}}{\text{MS}_{E}}$

- Reject $H_{0}$  at significance level $\alpha$ if $\text{TS} \ge F_{a-1, (a-1)(b-1), \alpha}$

** Sample means

- Assuming $a$ levels of $A$, $b$ levels of $B$, and $r$ data points in each group, you can imagine the dataset as a 3 dimensional array, $a$ rows (height), $b$ columns (width), and $r$ depth.
- Entry $X_{ijk}$ is the entry in row $i$, column $j$, depth $k$.
- Our model of the mean now can include an interaction term $\mu_{ij} = \mu + \alpha_{i} + \beta_{j} + \gamma_{ij}$, where $\gamma_{ij}$ represents additional change over the population mean due to interactions between the factors.
- In the notation below, we indicate a dimension being averaged/summed by replacing the index with "$\cdot$" .

  For example $X_{.j.}$ is the average for column $j$, averaging over all rows and samples that are in column $j$.
** Sample means

#+begin_export latex
\begin{align*}
X_{i..} &= \frac{1}{br}\sum_{j=1}^{b}\sum_{k=1}^{r}X_{ijk}\\
X_{.j.} &= \frac{1}{ar}\sum_{i=1}^{a}\sum_{k=1}^{r}X_{ijk}\\
X_{ij.} &= \frac{1}{r}\sum_{k=1}^{r}X_{ijk}\\
X_{...} &= \frac{1}{abr}\sum_{i=1}^{a}\sum_{j=1}^{b} \sum_{k=1}^{r}X_{ijk}
\end{align*}
#+end_export


** Sum of squares

Like the variance estimation for one-way ANOVA, and taking the ratio of two estimates of the variance, $\text{Var}_{A} = \frac{1}{a-1}\sum_{i=1}^{a}(X_{i..} - X_{...})^{2}$

#+begin_export latex
{\small
\begin{align*}
\text{SS}_{A} &= rb \sum_{i=1}^{a}(X_{i..} - X_{...})^{2}\\
\text{SS}_{B} &= ra \sum_{j=1}^{b}(X_{.j.} - X_{...})^{2}\\
\text{SS}_{AB} &= r \sum_{j=1}^{b} \sum_{i=1}^{a}(X_{ij.} - X_{i..} - X_{.j.} + X_{...})^{2}\\
\text{SS}_{E} &= \sum_{k=1}^{r} \sum_{j=1}^{b} \sum_{i=1}^{a}(X_{ijk} - X_{ij.})^{2}\\
\text{SS}_{total} &= \sum_{k=1}^{r} \sum_{j=1}^{b} \sum_{i=1}^{a}(X_{ijk} - X_{...})^{2}
\end{align*}
}
#+end_export

- $\text{SS}_{E}$ is also called /within treatment/ sum of squares

- It can be shown that $SS_{total} = SS_{A} + SS_{B} + SS_{AB} + SS_{E}$

** ANOVA table for $a\times b$ factorial experiment

For $N = abr$ data points,

|-------------+--------------+--------------+----------------------------------|
| Total       | $SS_{total}$ | $N - 1$      |                                  |
** ANOVA table for $a\times b$ factorial experiment: fixed effect, random effect, and mixed effect models

When the levels of each factor are chosen deliberately, it is called a *fixed effect model*. When they are chosen randomly from many possible levels, it is called a *random effect model*. When one factor has fixed levels, but the other one's levels are a random sample from all possible levels, it is called a *mixed effect model*.

In fixed effect model, $\alpha_{i}$, $\beta_{j}$ are fixed constants. In random effect model, they are random variables themselves.

** Calculating F-statistics for the hypotheses
1. $H_{0}:$ There is no interaction between A and B, i.e., the effect of one categorical variable does not depend on the other

   $F_{AB} = \frac{MS_{AB}}{MS_{E}}$ is distributed as $F_{(a-1)(b-1), N - ab}$

   Reject when $F_{AB} \ge F_{(a-1)(b-1), N - ab, \alpha}$

2. $H_{0}:$ There is no effect of factor A on the means, i.e., $\mu_{A_{1}} = \mu_{A_{2}} = \cdots = \mu_{A_{a}}$

   $F_{A} = \frac{MS_{A}}{MS_{E}}$ is distributed as $F_{a-1, N - ab}$

   Reject when $F_{A} \ge F_{a-1, N - ab, \alpha}$

3. $H_{0}:$ There is no effect of factor B on the means, i.e., $\mu_{B_{1}} = \mu_{B_{2}} = \cdots = \mu_{B_{b}}$

   $F_{B} = \frac{MS_{B}}{MS_{E}}$ is distributed as $F_{b-1, N - ab}$

   Reject when $F_{B} \ge F_{b-1, N - ab, \alpha}$

* Two-factor ANOVA in Python

** Two-factor ANOVA in Python
Calculations by hand are tedious. Doing 2-factor ANOVA in Python is easier.
#+LATEX: {\scriptsize
/Credit: The numbers for this example is adapted from [[https://milnepublishing.geneseo.edu/natural-resources-biometrics/chapter/chapter-6-two-way-analysis-of-variance/][Natural Resources Biometrics Copyright © 2014 by Diane Kiernan]]/

#+begin_src python  :exports code :results none
  import numpy as np
  from scipy   import stats
  data = np.array([ # Soil_1, Soil_2, Soil_3, Soil_4
        [[7.8, 9.1, 10.6], [11.2, 12.7, 13.3], [12.1, 12.5, 14.1], [9.1, 10.7, 12.6]],  # Strain 1
        [[8.0, 8.7, 10.0], [11.3, 12.9, 13.8], [13.8, 14.3, 15.4], [11.3, 12.7, 14.3]], # Strain 2
        [[15.3, 16.0, 17.6], [16.8, 18.3, 19.2], [17.9, 21.0, 20.7], [17.2, 18.3, 19.1]] # Strain 3
        ])
  print('Data:\n', data)
  print('Dimensions:', data.shape)
#+end_src
#+LATEX: }

Here the innermost dimension (third) is that samples for each pair of levels: $r=3$, the second dimension is the number of soil types, $b=4$ the first dimension is the number of strains $a = 3$.

** Two-factor ANOVA in Python
We can compute the means quite easily with ~numpy~ by specifying the axis
#+LATEX: {\scriptsize
#+begin_src python  :exports code :results none
  means_a = np.mean(data, axis=(1,2))
  means_b = np.mean(data, axis=(0,2))
  means_ab = np.mean(data, axis=2)
  grand_mean = np.mean(data)
  print(f'X_i..={means_a}\nX_.j.={means_b}\nX_..k={means_ab}\nX_...', grand_mean)
#+end_src
#+LATEX: }
** Two-factor ANOVA in Python
Then calculate the sum of squares for each factor
#+begin_src python  :exports code :results none
  a, b, r = data.shape
  ss_a = r * b * np.sum((means_a - grand_mean)**2)
  ss_b = r * a * np.sum((means_b - grand_mean)**2)
#+end_src

** Two-factor ANOVA in Python
To compute the sum of squares $\text{SS}_{AB}$ and $\text{SS}_{E}$ with numpy shortcuts, we need to match the dimensions of the arrays in the arithmetic operations. Otherwise you will get a broadcast error from numpy.
#+LATEX: {\small
#+begin_src python  :exports code :results none
  # Convert `means_a` with shape (3,) to shape (3,4) by repeating it
  #  as a column vector
  # `means_a[:,np.newaxis]` first converts it into 3x1 2D vector,
  # then `broadcast_to` tiles it to make `b` repeated columns
  ma_stacked = np.broadcast_to(means_a[:, np.newaxis], (a, b))
  # Convert `means_b` with shape (4,) to shape (3,4) by repeating it
  #  as a row vector
  # `means_b[np.newaxis,:]` first converts it into 1x4 2D vector,
  # then `broadcast_to` tiles it to make `a` repeated rows
  mb_stacked = np.broadcast_to(means_b[np.newaxis,:], (a, b))
  mab_stacked = np.broadcast_to(means_ab[:,:,np.newaxis], (a, b, r))
#+end_src
#+LATEX: }
** Two-factor ANOVA in Python
Now we can compute $\text{SS}_{AB}$ and $\text{SS}_{E}$ with the appropriately shaped arrays

#+begin_src python  :exports code :results none
    ss_ab = r * np.sum(
        (means_ab - ma_stacked - mb_stacked + grand_mean)**2)
    ss_e = np.sum((data - mab_stacked)**2)
#+end_src

** Two-factor ANOVA in Python
Then calculate the mean squares for each factor by dividing each SS by its dof.

#+begin_src python  :exports code :results none
  ms_a = ss_a / (a - 1)
  ms_b = ss_b / (b - 1)
  ms_ab = ss_ab / ((a - 1) * (b - 1))
  # Denominator dof - reused for all F stats
  dfe = (a * b * r - a * b)
  ms_e = ss_e / dfe
#+end_src

** Two-factor ANOVA in Python
Now we can compute the F-values:

#+begin_src python  :exports code :results none
  F_ab = ms_ab / ms_e
  F_a = ms_a / ms_e
  F_b = ms_b / ms_e
#+end_src

** Two-factor ANOVA in Python
And finally, test our hypotheses
#+LATEX: {\scriptsize
#+begin_src python  :exports code :results none
  alpha = 0.05
  F_crit_ab = stats.f.ppf(1 - alpha, (a - 1) * (b - 1), dfe)
  F_crit_a = stats.f.ppf(1 - alpha, a - 1, dfe)
  F_crit_b = stats.f.ppf(1 - alpha, b - 1, dfe)

  p_ab = stats.f.sf(F_ab, (a - 1) * (b - 1), dfe)
  p_a = stats.f.sf(F_a, a - 1, dfe)
  p_b = stats.f.sf(F_b, b - 1, dfe)

  print(f'Interaction: F={F_ab:.4f}, F_crit={F_crit_ab:.4f},'
        f' p={p_ab:.6f}, reject H0={F_ab >= F_crit_ab}')
  print(f'Factor A:    F={F_a:.4f}, F_crit={F_crit_a:.4f},'
        f' p={p_a:.6f}, reject H0={F_a >= F_crit_a}')
  print(f'Factor B:    F={F_b:.4f}, F_crit={F_crit_b:.4f},'
        f' p={p_b:.6f}, reject H0={F_b >= F_crit_b}')
#+end_src
#+LATEX: }
** Two-factor ANOVA in Python with ~statsmodels~ module
It is simpler to do this analysis using the ~statsmodels~ package. However, ~statsmodels~ expects dataframes like excel spreadsheets or tables in R. Thus we have to unwrap our 3-dimensional array into rows and columns (~rows~ in the code below) and make a ~pandas DataFrame~ out of it.

#+LATEX: {\scriptsize
#+begin_src python  :exports code :results none
  import pandas as pd
  from statsmodels.formula.api import ols
  from statsmodels.stats.anova import anova_lm

  # Reshape the (a, b, r) array into long format: one row per observation
  rows = [(f'strain_{i+1}', f'soil_{j+1}', data[i, j, k])
          for i in range(a) for j in range(b) for k in range(r)]
  df = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=['strain', 'soil', 'value'])
  formula = 'value ~ C(strain) + C(soil) + C(strain):C(soil)'
  model = ols(formula, data=df).fit()
  print(anova_lm(model, typ=2))
#+end_src
#+LATEX: }

** Notes on ~statsmodels~ 2-F ANOVA
Note the line
#+begin_src python  :exports code :results none :tangle no
  formula = 'value ~ C(strain) + C(soil) + C(strain):C(soil)'
#+end_src
- This is another way of saying $\mu_{ij} = \mu + \alpha_{i} + \beta_{j} + \gamma_{ij}$
- ~value~ is dependent on ~strain~ and ~soil~, and their interaction
- ~C(strain)~ tells ~statsmodels~ to treat ~strain~ as a categorical variable, and same for ~C(soil)~. Since our labels are already strings, ~statsmodels~ would have treated them as categorical anyway, even without ~C(...)~ - the wrapper only actually matters when the labels are numeric, in which case ~statsmodels~ would otherwise fit a line assuming these to be coordinates.
- ~C(strain):C(soil)~ tells ~statsmodels~ to include the interaction between the two factors in the model.
- A compact form of the formula is: ~'value ~ C(strain)*C(soil)'~

** Notes on ~statsmodels~ 2-F ANOVA
Note the line
#+begin_src python  :exports code :results none :tangle no
    model = ols(formula, data=df).fit()
#+end_src
- ~ols~ stands for "ordinary least squares", where the squared difference between the dependent variable value and the value computed with the formula using the corresponding values of the independent variables is minimized to estimate the coefficients in the formula. We actually fit a linear regression model to the data (this is related to general linear model, or GLM) using this method

** Notes on ~statsmodels~ 2-F ANOVA
~anova_lm(model, typ)~
- ~model~ is a fitted linear model (output of ~ols(...).fit()~)
- ~typ~ can be ~1~, ~2~, or ~3~. Does not matter for balanced design with insignificant interaction.
  - ~typ=1~: /Sequential sums of squares/ - the sum of squares are computed incrementally, following the sequence in your formula. Not suited for unbalanced design unless there is a known hierarchy/order of the terms reflected in their sequence in the formula
  - ~typ=2~: Appropriate when interactions can be ignored - should be default
  - ~typ=3~: Appropriate for significant interaction, /but it's complicated/
  - Read more at [[https://www.r-bloggers.com/2011/03/anova-%e2%80%93-type-iiiiii-ss-explained/][R-bloggers]] and [[https://stats.stackexchange.com/questions/345684/which-one-to-choose-type-i-type-ii-or-type-iii-anova][stats.stackexchange]]
