* Theory
** Weak Law of large numbers
Let $X_{1}, X_{2}, \cdots X_{N}$ be iid random variables each having finite expected value $\mu$. Then for any $\epsilon > 0$, $\lim_{N \to \infty} P(|\bar{X} - \mu | \ge \epsilon) = 0$
** Central limit theorem (CLT)
Let $X_{1}, X_{2}, \cdots X_{N}$ be iid random variables each having finite expected value $\mu$ and finite nonzero variance $\sigma^{2}$. Then the random variable $Z = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{N}}$ converges in distribution to the standard normal random variable as $N \to \infty$

Thus whenever you are looking at a random variable that results as a summation of many iid random variables, it tends to be distributed normally.
** Continuous random variables
- The distribution of a continuous random variable is defined by a function $\mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$
- This is called /probability density function/ (PDF) as opposed to /probability mass function/ for discrete rv
- The probability at any single point on the real line is 0. We can only talk about probability of the rv being within a range of values $P(a \le X \le b)$ which is the area under the curve between $a$ and $b$ in the plot of the /probability density function/
- The total area under a PDF must be 1
** Normal or Gaussian random variable
- The PDF is determined by two /parameters/ - mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$
- Bell-shaped curve symmetrical around $\mu$, the spread determined by $\sigma$
  - $P(X < \mu) = P(X > \mu) = 1/2$
- The height at point $x$ is $\frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{(x - \mu)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}$
  - Not every bell-shaped symmetric curve is normal
- A normal distribution with mean 0 and SD 1 is called standard normal, usually represented by $Z$
* Useful definitions and results
** Normal to standard normal
- Any normal random variable can be /standardized/ to make a standard normal distribution
- $Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}$
- By plotting the data in standard normal form you can get a quick feel of the data
** Approximate rule for normal distribution
- $P(\mu - \sigma < X < \mu + \sigma) \approx 0.68$
  - 68% of values lie within one SD around mean
- $P(\mu - 2 \sigma < X < \mu + 2 \sigma) \approx 0.95$
  - 95% of values lie within two SD around mean
- $P(\mu - 3 \sigma < X < \mu + 3 \sigma) \approx 0.997$
  - 99.7% of values lie within three SD around mean
** Some useful results
- $P(Z > x) = 1 - P(Z \le x)$
- $P(-a < Z < a) = 2P(Z < a) - 1$
- The sum of two independent normal rvs is also a normal rv
  - $E(X + Y) = E(X) + E(Y) = \mu_{x} + \mu_{y}$
  - $Var(X + Y) = Var(X) + Var(Y) = {\sigma_{x}}^{2} + {\sigma_{y}}^{2}$
