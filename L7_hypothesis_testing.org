** Hypothesis in /statistics/
“A statistical hypothesis is a statement about the nature of a population. It is often stated in terms of a population parameter.” - Sheldon M. Ross (Introductory Statistics)
- Null hypothesis, H0, is the claim we want to disprove.
- Alternative hypothesis, H1, is what we are actually trying to establish.

** Example of hypothesis
- You discovered a drug “Miraculin” for COVID-19
- You want to show that it cures the disease
- H0: Miraculin has no effect on COVID test result.
- H1: After treatment with Miraculin, patients test negative for COVID.
- You want to reject the null hypothesis.

*In (empirical) science you never prove anything. You do not prove the alternate hypothesis.*

** Rejecting or /failing to reject/ the null hypothesis
- Rejecting H0 is a strong statement that it does not appear to be consistent with observed data.
- Failing to reject H0 is a weak statement interpreted to mean that H0 is consistent with the data.
- Decision errors:
  - (I) Rejecting H0 when it is actually true. (probability=$\alpha$)
  - (II) Failing to reject H0 when H1 is actually true. (probability=$\beta$)

* Z-test for proportions

** Test statistic
- "A /test statistic/ is a statistic whose value is determined from the sample data. Depending on the value of this test statistic, the null hypothesis will be rejected or not."

- "The /critical region/, also called the /rejection region/, is that set of values of the test statistic for which the null hypothesis is rejected."

Sheldon M. Ross

** Significance level
- We fix a small significance level $\alpha$ and then require that the $P(\text{Reject H0 when H0 is true}) \le \alpha$

* Hypotheis test for normal population

** Test for the mean of a normal population with /known variance/
The population variance $\sigma^{2}$ is known, and we want to check if the population mean $\mu$ is equal to a specified value $\mu_{0}$ or not

- H0: $\mu = \mu_{0}$
- H1: $\mu \ne \mu_{0}$

** Critical region
- $\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}$
- If the sample mean is far away from $\mu_{0}$ we can reject H0
- The critical region of the test $C = |\bar{X} - \mu_{0}| \ge c$ for some value $c$
- For a significance level $\alpha$: when H0 is true, the probability that $\bar{X}$ differs from $\mu_{0}$ by $c$ or more
  - $P(|\bar{X} - \mu_{0}| \ge c) = \alpha$ when $\mu = \mu_{0}$

** Z-test for proportions
- True proportion = $p$
- Sample proportion = $\hat{p}$
- For Bernoulli trial $\sigma^{2} = p (1 - p)$
  - When $n$ is large enough that both $np$ and $n(1-p)$ are $> 5$, we can use the normal approximation to the binomial distribution.
  - SEM = $\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p})}{n}}$
